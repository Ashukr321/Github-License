# Creative Commons Zero v1.0 Universal (CC0-1.0)

## 📜 Overview

**Creative Commons Zero v1.0 Universal (CC0-1.0)** is not technically a license — it's a **public domain dedication**. By using CC0, the author **waives all copyright and related rights** to the maximum extent permitted by law, effectively placing the work in the **public domain**. It means "no rights reserved."

---

## 📝 Key Provisions

```
CC0 1.0 Universal (CC0 1.0) Public Domain Dedication

The person who associated a work with this deed has dedicated the
work to the public domain by waiving all of his or her rights to
the work worldwide under copyright law, including all related and
neighboring rights, to the extent allowed by law.

You can copy, modify, distribute and perform the work, even for
commercial purposes, all without asking permission.
```

> 📖 Full text: [CC0 1.0](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/legalcode)

## 🔑 Key Characteristics

- **Public Domain**: Not a license — it's a dedication to public domain
- **Zero Conditions**: Absolutely no requirements for users
- **No Attribution**: Users don't even need to credit the author
- **Universal**: Designed to work in all legal jurisdictions
- **Fallback License**: In jurisdictions where public domain dedication isn't possible, CC0 includes a permissive fallback license
- **Not for Software (Recommended)**: Creative Commons recommends using MIT/Apache for software, CC0 for data and content

---

## ⚠️ Important Notes

### CC0 vs MIT
```
MIT: "Do whatever you want, just keep my name on it"
CC0: "Do whatever you want. Period. Forget I exist."
```

### Software vs Content
Creative Commons **officially recommends**:
- **CC0 for data, datasets, and content** (images, text, music)
- **MIT/Apache for software** (code, applications)

However, some notable software projects (like SQLite) do use CC0/public domain.
