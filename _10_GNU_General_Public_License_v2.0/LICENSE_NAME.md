# GNU General Public License v2.0 (GPL-2.0)

## 📜 Overview

The **GNU General Public License v2.0 (GPL-2.0)** is the predecessor to GPL v3.0 and remains one of the most historically significant open-source licenses. Released on **June 1991** by the **Free Software Foundation**, it established the **copyleft** principle that changed the software world. Many major projects still use GPL v2.0, most notably the **Linux Kernel**.

## 🔑 GPL v2.0 vs GPL v3.0 — Key Differences

| Feature              | GPL v2.0          | GPL v3.0              |
| -------------------- | ----------------- | --------------------- |
| Anti-Tivoization     | ❌ No             | ✅ Yes                |

## ⚠️ The "v2 Only" vs "v2 or Later" Debate

```
"GPL-2.0-only":    Licensed exclusively under GPL v2.0
                   (Linux Kernel uses this)

"GPL-2.0-or-later": Licensed under GPL v2.0 OR any later version
                   (allows upgrading to GPL v3.0+)
```

### Why Linux Stays GPL v2.0
Linus Torvalds intentionally chose **"GPL-2.0-only"** because:
- He disagreed with some GPL v3.0 provisions (anti-tivoization)
- Thousands of contributors would need to agree to relicense
- GPL v2.0 already serves the kernel's needs
- "I chose GPL v2 because I think it's the best license out there" — Linus
